# Getting paid from abroad

What actually works for a Pakistan-registered company, and where the crossover
between methods sits.

> Fees and availability change. Check current terms with the provider before
> quoting anything to a client.

---

## The realistic options

### Direct SWIFT wire

Works into any major Pakistani bank. Typically two to five business days,
sometimes longer for a first payment from a new client while compliance checks
run at either end.

**Cost shape:** a flat charge, usually USD 15–50 deducted in transit by
correspondent banks, plus the exchange spread when your bank converts.

**Best for:** larger invoices. The flat fee becomes trivial as the amount rises,
and the money arrives directly in the company account coded as an export receipt.

**What clients need from you:** beneficiary name exactly as the bank holds it,
beneficiary address, bank name, branch, IBAN, account number, SWIFT/BIC,
currency, and a payment reference. Confirm the SWIFT code and any intermediary
bank details **with your own branch in writing** before publishing them —
several Pakistani banks route inbound differently from their headline BIC, and a
wire sent on a guessed code can sit in an investigation queue for a week.

### Payment platforms

Virtual receiving accounts in USD, EUR and GBP let an overseas client pay by a
domestic transfer in their own country rather than originating an international
wire. That is a genuine advantage when you are asking someone to pay a Pakistani
company for the first time — it removes the friction and the unfamiliarity.

**Cost shape:** a percentage on receipt, typically around 1% when the client
pays by bank transfer and materially more when they pay by card, plus a
conversion markup of roughly 2% when you withdraw to your Pakistani account.

**Best for:** smaller invoices, first-time clients, and clients who find
international wires intimidating.

**Two things to get right.** Open the account in the **company's** name, not
your personal name, and make the account title match your bank account title
character for character — a mismatch is one of the most common causes of stuck
withdrawals. And apply early: approval takes days, and you do not want it on the
critical path when a client is ready to pay.

### Cards

Not available to Pakistan-registered businesses through the major processors.
Access requires a foreign entity, which creates filing obligations and tax
exposure in that jurisdiction and moves revenue outside the Pakistani export
framework — which can put your concessional treatment at risk. Revisit only if
you develop genuine recurring card-billed revenue, and take cross-border tax
advice first.

## The crossover

Percentage-based platform pricing and flat wire fees cross over somewhere around
**USD 3,000–5,000**, depending on current rates.

- **Under USD 3,000** — platform payment request. Cheaper and much easier for the
  client.
- **Over USD 5,000** — bank wire, details on the invoice. A USD 5,000 wire might
  cost USD 50–80 all in, against USD 150–250 through a percentage-based platform.
- **Between the two** — put both on the invoice and let the client's finance team
  pick the one they already use.

Never make a first-time client learn a new payment method to pay you. The
friction costs more than the fee.

## The cost nobody itemises

When foreign currency is converted to rupees, your bank does not use the
interbank rate. It uses its own buying rate, which sits below it. The difference
is the bank's margin, it appears nowhere on your statement, and it is typically
**larger than every explicit charge on the transaction combined**.

Three things reduce it:

1. **Hold foreign currency and spend it as foreign currency.** Money you convert
   to rupees and later spend on hosting, tooling or an overseas subcontractor has
   been converted twice, paying the spread both ways. An exporter's foreign
   currency account, spent directly on foreign costs, is converted zero times.
2. **Ask for a rate before large conversions.** On a five-figure receipt, ask the
   branch what rate they are applying and whether treasury can improve it. Banks
   do give better rates on volume to customers who ask, and never volunteer it.
3. **Consolidate.** Four payments of USD 2,500 pay four sets of flat charges. One
   payment of USD 10,000 pays one. Structure milestones with this in mind.

## What you cannot do

Receive export proceeds into a personal account to get a better rate, or hold
earnings offshore waiting for the rupee to move. The first breaks the
requirement that export income be received in the business account through
banking channels; the second risks the repatriation condition that the
concessional treatment depends on.

The optimisation available to you is all inside the framework: the retention
account, asking for a rate, and consolidating payments.
