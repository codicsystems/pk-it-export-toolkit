# First 90 days

Registration steps in dependency order. Each one produces a document the next
one needs, and attempting them out of order is the most common reason this takes
three months instead of three weeks.

> Deadlines and requirements change. Confirm each against the relevant authority
> at the time you act. See [DISCLAIMER.md](../DISCLAIMER.md).

---

## Phase 1 — the company exists on paper

- [ ] **Reserve the name and incorporate** through the SECP portal. You receive a
      Certificate of Incorporation and a CUIN.
- [ ] **File the registered office** with the registrar. There is a statutory
      deadline measured from incorporation, and until it is filed your company
      may have no registered office on the public register — which both banks and
      PSEB will pull.
- [ ] **Deposit the subscription money.** The share capital you subscribed for
      must actually be paid into a company bank account within the prescribed
      period, and reported to the registrar with a certificate from a practising
      chartered accountant or cost and management accountant. The sanction for
      missing this is not just a fine — shares can be treated as cancelled.
- [ ] **Execute a tenancy or occupancy agreement** for the registered office. A
      written agreement on stamp paper naming the *company* as tenant at a stated
      rent. A letter of permission is not a tenancy agreement, and PSEB will not
      accept one from a company.

> **Order matters here.** The office filing has to be done before the bank and
> PSEB pull your register entry, and the subscription money cannot be deposited
> before there is an account to deposit it into. Sequence: file the office →
> open the account → deposit → certify.

## Phase 2 — the company exists to the tax authority

- [ ] **Company NTN.** Registration on the tax portal using the CUIN. Because the
      registrar and the tax authority are integrated, this may be generated
      automatically at incorporation — check before you apply, you may already
      have it.
- [ ] **Check the declared principal activity** matches the object clause in your
      Memorandum. PSEB rejects applications where the declared activity does not
      clearly state IT, and a mismatch between your two registrations is slow to
      unpick.
- [ ] **Correct your personal tax record** if its address or details do not match
      the company's city. A company in one city whose sole director's tax record
      points at another generates questions from bank compliance officers and
      PSEB reviewers that cost weeks.
- [ ] **Confirm you appear on the Active Taxpayers List** and stay there. File
      the annual return even in a year where all income is covered by a final tax
      — not filing because "there was no tax to pay" removes you from the list,
      which breaks PSEB eligibility, which breaks the concessional rate.

## Phase 3 — the company can receive money

- [ ] **Open the corporate bank account.** This is the long pole; start it the
      day the NTN issues. Take the full document set: incorporation certificate,
      Memorandum and Articles, incorporation application and office filing, NTN
      certificate, your CNIC and personal NTN, a board resolution authorising the
      account and naming the signatory, company letterhead, and the tenancy
      agreement even if they do not ask.
- [ ] **Request the account maintenance letter** at opening. PSEB requires it and
      it is easy to forget, then wait a week for. Ask for two originals.
- [ ] **File the purpose code declaration** and get the remittance officer's
      name. [Template here](../templates/purpose-code-declaration.md).
- [ ] **Confirm in writing** which SWIFT code and intermediary bank the branch
      wants used for inbound foreign currency, before you publish it on an
      invoice.
- [ ] **Open a payment platform account in the company name**, matching the bank
      account title character for character. Approval takes days — do it before a
      client is waiting to pay.

> **What goes wrong here.** Compliance officers are cautious with newly
> incorporated single-member companies, particularly with a residential
> registered address. Everything in Phases 1 and 2 exists partly to defuse that.
> If a branch stalls for more than two weeks, go to a different branch from a
> published list of IT-sector facilitation branches rather than escalating within
> the same one.

## Phase 4 — the company is a recognised exporter

- [ ] **Register with PSEB.** The single highest-return registration available.
      Documents: company NTN, SECP-certified incorporation documents, director
      CNIC, bank account maintenance letter, and the tenancy agreement. Select the
      IT company category, not freelancer and not call centre — the wrong category
      is a listed cause of delay.
- [ ] **Open the exporters' foreign currency account** once PSEB issues. Ask for
      three things in one conversation: the account, written confirmation of the
      retention percentage they will apply, and a debit card against the balance
      for paying foreign costs.
- [ ] **Register on the government e-procurement portal** as a vendor if domestic
      public sector work is of interest. Free, and eligibility is checked
      automatically against your company and tax records — which is why everything
      above needs to be clean first.
- [ ] **Diary the PSEB renewal** the day the certificate issues. A lapse means the
      concessional rate does not apply to income received during the gap.

## Phase 5 — ongoing

- [ ] **Appoint the first auditor** within the statutory period from
      incorporation.
- [ ] **Engage a compliance retainer** — a company secretary or small firm
      handling the annual return, statutory registers and reminders. For a single
      founder running a business, this is the difference between compliance being
      handled and compliance being the thing you discover you missed.
- [ ] Work through [`compliance-calendar.md`](compliance-calendar.md) and put
      every recurring obligation in a calendar with two reminders.

---

## Realistic timeline

Four to six weeks from incorporation to a fully compliant, registered, exporting
company **if you drive it**. Three months if you wait for each step to remind
you. The bank account is the long pole in almost every case.
