# Every time money arrives from abroad

Four things. They take about ten minutes and they are the difference between
having proof of export income and having a bank statement.

---

### 1. Check the purpose code on the credit advice

Ask the bank for the credit advice for the payment, and look at the purpose code
it was realised under.

- **Correct** — it identifies information technology or IT-enabled services.
- **Wrong** — it identifies a home remittance, personal transfer or gift.

If it is wrong, call the branch **the same week**. Corrections are administrative
within days and a negotiation within months. Reference your purpose code
declaration and the officer you originally spoke to.

Keep a running log. If the same branch codes two payments wrong, escalate above
the branch — banks are required to operate a complaint mechanism for IT
exporters, which gives you a route that is not just another conversation at the
counter.

### 2. Ask for the Proceeds Realisation Certificate

Also issued as an Inward Remittance Certificate depending on the bank.

Many banks generate it automatically once a remittance is credited under an
export code — and many will not tell you, and some will not issue one unless you
ask by name. **Ask by name.**

This certificate is your legal proof of export income. You will need it for:

- Tax assessment, as evidence of what your export income was
- PSEB renewal, where turnover is verified
- Visa applications, where a genuine trading income is exactly what is being
  assessed

A remittance credited under a personal code still produces a certificate. It
just does not support the claim you need it to support.

### 3. File the four documents together

One folder per client per year, backed up. For each payment:

- The **invoice** you sent
- The client's **remittance advice**, if they sent one
- The **bank credit advice**
- The **PRC / IRC**

This must exist from invoice one, not from the point you first need it. It is
close to impossible to reconstruct two years later, and it is the first thing
asked for in any assessment.

### 4. Record what actually landed

Not just the invoice amount. Record:

- The **amount received** in foreign currency
- The **exchange rate the bank actually gave you** — not the interbank rate you
  saw in the news
- **Bank charges** deducted, including anything taken in transit by a
  correspondent bank
- **Tax withheld** at source
- The **net** that reached the account

The gap between the interbank rate and the rate your bank applies is usually the
largest single cost on an inbound payment, and it is not itemised anywhere. If
you do not record it you will never know what your effective cost of getting
paid is.

---

## Monthly, not per payment

**Withdraw platform balances to your Pakistani bank account.** If you receive
through Payoneer or a similar platform, do not let the balance accumulate.
Concessional treatment of export income is conditional on bringing proceeds into
Pakistan through banking channels, and money sitting offshore puts that at risk
across your whole export book — not just the part left abroad.

Make it a monthly habit whether or not you need the money.
